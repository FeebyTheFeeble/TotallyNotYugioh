# Duel Prototype — Design Specification

## Product goal

Build a PC-first, single-player card-game prototype centered on the tactical rhythm of a 5D-era Synchro duel. The prototype is immediately playable from the existing Unity sample scene and includes a turn-by-turn action prompt, a deterministic rules core, a fast opponent, collection/deck editing, randomized booster purchases, and an in-Editor card-authoring workflow.

The runtime catalog is imported from `Assets/Resources/all_ygo_cards.csv`, a user-supplied snapshot containing 14,137 uniquely identified cards. Printed names, stats, card categories, attributes, and effect text are retained. The duel engine does not yet implement every imported card effect; the automated report distinguishes imported text from behavior that is actually scripted.

## Experience and screens

The navigation header highlights the currently selected screen.

1. **Duel** — The initial screen. Shows a clickable left-to-right phase track (Draw, Standby, Main 1, Battle, Main 2, End), each phase color-coded and the active phase outlined, both players' prominent Life Points, card-shaped Deck and Graveyard counters, distinct AI and player portrait placeholders, five centered Monster Zones per player, mirrored Spell/Trap rows, dedicated Field Spell Zones, and the player's hand. Monster and Spell/Trap zones share aligned columns, with extra spacing to accommodate sideways Defense Position cards. A gold portrait outline and CURRENT TURN label identify the active player and switch sides when the turn changes. The human Deck counter sits left of the Spell/Trap row and its Graveyard counter sits to the right; these positions are reversed for the opponent, whose Deck is at top right and Graveyard at top left. Clicking either Deck counter opens a scrollable list of the remaining cards with a visible vertical scrollbar, grouped by name and showing each card's remaining copies and probability of appearing on the next draw. Selecting a list entry shows its card preview, type, stats, and effect in a panel on the right; hovering a card also shows its preview. Clicking a Graveyard counter opens a scrollable inspection overlay with card details. Empty zones are labeled by type (Monster Slot, Spell/Trap Slot, or Field Slot). Clicking a later phase advances through intervening phases; clicking End again passes the turn. All on-mat card slots use matching 5:7 portrait proportions and shared, evenly spaced columns on both player sides; hand cards use the same proportion, are centered, and use compact spacing. The Extra Deck is represented by a separate box that opens a card-selection popup; hovering a card shows its preview and selecting one closes the popup. Hovering over a card shows its type, stats, and effect in the card preview; there is no separate selected-card effect panel. The human monsters sit above their Spell/Trap row; the opponent's monsters sit below theirs. Card slots and previews reserve a portrait 5:7 card-art area; generated placeholder art is used until licensed assets are supplied. Selecting a hand monster offers Normal Summon and Set; selecting a Spell offers its available activation/Set actions. The prompt names the active phase, next useful action, and opening-turn attack restriction.
2. **Deck** — A two-column builder shows the current Main and Extra Deck lists on the left and a searchable Card Library on the right. Filter the library by card type and sort by name, ATK, Level, or type; results are paged to keep the large imported catalog responsive. Each result shows ownership and deck-copy counts and can be added directly. Remove copies from the deck list. Hovering a card opens its full effect-text preview. The current duel engine requires at least 40 Main Deck cards; the Editor structure-deck validator allows 40–60. Copies are limited to three per card, and Extra Deck cards are limited to 15.
3. **Shop** — Purchase five-card randomized packs, shown as 5:7 portrait packs without overlapping placeholder text, with in-game credits. Buying opens a reveal overlay showing all five pulled cards; card acquisition updates the collection, and credits and deck/collection state persist locally.
4. **Cards** — Browse the imported catalog in a paged, names-only list. A type dropdown filters Normal/Effect Monsters, known Tuners, Synchro/Fusion/Xyz/Link Monsters, and supported Spell/Trap subtypes. The selected card appears in a portrait 5:7 preview with its type, stats, and imported effect text; placeholder art has no overlaid lettering. Hidden opposing cards do not reveal their identity.
5. **AI Lab** — Select two structure decks and run AI-vs-AI duels on the same board layout as a regular duel, including both Monster rows, Spell/Trap rows, Field Spell Zones, Decks, and Graveyards. Portraits identify the active seat; the center toolbar supports step, autoplay pace, pause, hand inspection, deck selection, and reset. The Hands overlay shows the first bot's cards face-up and the opposing bot's cards as backs, with horizontally scrollable rows. The board also shows both Life Point totals, hand counts, phase, and recent actions.
6. **Card Studio (Unity Editor)** — Create CardDefinition assets and validate IDs, types, levels, effect values and duplicate catalog identifiers.

The AI Lab includes four 40-card Synchro-era themed presets: Junk Synchron, Blackwing, Stardust Synchron, and Morphtronic Power Tool. Each Main Deck obeys the three-copy limit and each preset includes an Extra Deck. They are starter lists, not official product decklists.

## Duel loop

- Both players start at 8000 Life Points, draw five cards, and draw at the start of each turn.
- A turn enters Draw, Standby, Main Phase 1, Battle, Main Phase 2, then End. The player can click a later phase to advance through intervening phases; clicking End again passes play to the opponent.
- Main Phases allow one Normal Summon/Set, Spell activation, and a Synchro Summon. Level 5–6 monsters require one Tribute and Level 7+ require two.
- A Set monster enters face-down Defense Position, displayed sideways. Face-up Defense Position monsters are also displayed sideways. A Set monster can be Flip Summoned face-up in Attack Position during a later turn's Main Phase; monsters summoned this turn cannot change position. A face-down monster attacked in Defense is revealed before damage calculation and remains face-up in Defense if it survives.
- A Synchro Summon consumes face-up monsters you control: at least one Tuner and one or more non-Tuners, with their Levels adding exactly to the chosen Synchro Monster's Level. The SDMY sample also supports Polymerization Fusion Summons from hand/field using the listed prototype material recipes, plus De-Fusion for Fusion Monsters summoned by this method.
- A monster can attack once per turn. Attacks compare ATK to ATK or DEF according to battle position; destroying a Defense Position monster does not inflict battle damage to its controller, while a stronger Defense stat inflicts the difference to the attacking player. Face-up monster slots display both ATK and DEF. Direct attacks are available only when the opposing field is clear. Neither player may attack during the duel's first turn.
- Life Points reaching zero or a required draw from an empty Deck ends the duel.
- A human duel displays a results overlay with the winner, turn count, remaining Life Points, full duel event log, and a prominent Rematch button.
- The opponent uses the same core summon, effect, Synchro/Fusion, phase and battle rules. Its board-aware heuristic prefers summons that enable Synchro plays, preserves valuable Tuners/materials when choosing Tributes, sequences draw/removal/revival and useful effects ahead of combat, and attacks only when the exchange or direct damage is favorable.
- Sangan's field-to-Graveyard trigger searches its controller's Deck for a Main Deck monster with 1500 or less ATK. A human controller receives a mandatory scrollable candidate list, a full card preview, and an explicit confirmation action; the AI selects a legal monster automatically. The search is once per turn, and the chosen card name's effects are locked for the rest of that turn.
- Set Traps have event hooks for attacks, Spell activations, monster-effect activations, and Trap links. The AI turn pauses for a human response window when a Set Trap can respond; the player can activate an eligible Trap or pass. Trap responses are logged as chain links, and Counter Traps can answer a Trap link. The AI activates eligible Counter Traps automatically.
- Original sample effects include draw-two, Graveyard revival in Defense Position, attack cancellation, Spell/monster-effect negation, and a Counter Trap that can negate another Trap link. Quick-Play Spells can be used from hand during the player's Main, Battle, or End Phase. Continuous and Field Spells refresh their modeled effect on their controller's turns; Field Spells occupy their own zone and replace the previous Field Spell. Equip Spells grant a persistent ATK bonus and are sent to the Graveyard if their monster leaves the field. The expanded sample catalog adds original effect monsters, draw/removal Spells, attack-stopping Traps, Counter Traps, and a Field Spell.
- The AI Lab runs both seats through the same rules core and advances one full turn per Step action. Autoplay defaults to a 10-second pause between turns and can be adjusted to 5, 10, or 15 seconds so viewers can follow the match; it stops at the duel result.
- The scripted subset includes Sangan's search trigger; Electromagnet Warrior summon triggers; Berserkion/Valkyrion summon and field effects; the Dark Paladin Spell-negation discard response; several Dark Magician/Magnet Warrior spells; Mirror Force/Magic Cylinder responses; and timed Swords effects. Mystical Space Typhoon also provides an explicit Spell/Trap target choice. Fusion Summoning and De-Fusion are wired through the duel UI and AI for the prototype material recipes. Other SDMY effects—including multiple Fusion Monster passives/triggers, some monster passives, and several Trap triggers—remain explicitly marked as incomplete in their card summaries.

## Architecture

- **Data:** `CardCatalogCsvImporter` reads the user-supplied CSV from Unity Resources and constructs `CardDefinition` records. The importer preserves source text and raw ATK/DEF display values, maps card-frame categories, and marks a curated set of Synchro-era Tuners used by the starter presets. Additional editor-authored assets can be loaded from `Resources/CardCatalog`.
- **Rules:** `DuelEngine` owns player zones, phases, action validation, effect dispatch, battle resolution, turn transitions and winner checks. UI code requests actions; it does not mutate duel state directly.
- **Presentation/application:** the runtime UI is generated from code so the prototype works in the sample scene without manual hierarchy wiring.
- All runtime scroll views support mouse-wheel scrolling, including horizontal card-hand rows.
- **AI test harness:** named deck presets are supplied by the catalog, while the duel engine exposes a single-turn AI stepping method for both interactive and automated matches.
- **Mock Duel Test Lab:** the in-game Test Duel tab lets a player search effect cards, seed a focused card into hand, choose optional field/Graveyard fixtures, and play a normal duel against the CPU. The user can mark a manual pass/fail and restart setup; automated EditMode tests remain the authoritative assertions.
- **Persistence/economy:** a small local profile stores credits, collection counts and the chosen deck; pack rolls use catalog weights.
- **Extension seams:** supported prototype effects are enumerated and resolved centrally; importing a card's effect text does not register behavior. New effect behavior requires an explicit resolver and mock-duel regression test. The catalog coverage test fails when an effect-bearing imported card has no resolver; `Card-Effect-Mock-Duel-Report.md` marks those cards as effect failures instead of passing them as data-only cases.

## Prototype boundaries and next steps

This is a playable foundation, not a complete tournament rules simulator. The CSV import provides broad card discovery and printed text, but most imported effects are not implemented by the duel engine; the report marks those explicitly rather than treating text import as a passing behavior test. The response/chain system does not model every activation condition, timing restriction, or official chain interaction. Some summon procedures and card-specific behaviors may be partial; verify the engine, runtime UI, AI, and tests for each mechanic instead of inferring support from catalog types. The EditMode suite includes card-attributed behavior tests, but a passing report is not evidence that every imported card effect works.
